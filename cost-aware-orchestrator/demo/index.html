The power-boundary law

A watt figure is meaningless without the boundary it was taken at. These are not interchangeable, and energy-per-token must never be ranked across them:

  • whole_device — everything the wall or barrel jack supplies (IQ9: 18.3 W).
  • gpu_rail — accelerator only; excludes host CPU, host DRAM and PSU loss (RTX 5090: 286 W).
  • module_rail — Jetson module via tegrastats; excludes the carrier board. Jetson never reports through nvidia-smi.
  • unavailable — no current channel exists in hardware. The QCS9075 is in this class: it exposes thermals, devfreq, cpufreq and perf, and no power rail at all.

Comparing an 18.3 W whole-device number against a 286 W GPU-rail number as if they were the same quantity is the single error this project guards against hardest.

Accuracy did not collapse to buy that latency. On JSON-mode the IQ9 scored 1.00 against the RTX 5090's 0.92 — the edge NPU was the more accurate of the two on its winning shape. On GSM8K it scored 0.75 against 0.80: a real but modest deficit on the shape it also loses on latency. measured

Why the split falls this way

Short, structured, prefill-dominated work (JSON, tool calls) plays to the Hexagon NPU's fast prefill and low fixed overhead. Long autoregressive generation (chain-of-thought arithmetic) is decode-bound, and decode is where a 286 W discrete GPU's memory bandwidth is simply larger. The practical consequence is a routing rule, not a winner: send the agentic glue to the NPU and the long reasoning to the GPU.

A correction we publish rather than bury

An earlier internal conclusion — “8B does not fit the Hexagon NPU; 4B is the ceiling” — was wrong, and is retracted. The failures were a serving-path and board-state artifact, not a hardware capacity limit: one code path cannot load the 8B and kills its own server process while doing so, while the direct inference path loads and runs it normally on a freshly booted, idle NPU. Three independent clean-boot runs confirm the 8B works.

The general lesson is the one this whole project is organised around: a claim about a live system is not confirmed until the experiment that would falsify it has actually run. The original conclusion had been double-checked by two reviewers on correct arithmetic — and was still wrong, because nobody had rebooted the board and tried again.

What the IQ9 does NOT win: energy per token

IQ9: 0.97 tok/J. GB10 leads at roughly 1.48 tok/J. derived — and note these two sit at different power boundaries, so even that comparison is indicative rather than a ranking. An earlier “IQ9 is the energy leader at 1.48 tok/J” claim came from an optimistic ~12 W power proxy; once the board was metered at 18.3 W the figure fell to 0.97 and the energy-leadership claim was withdrawn.

What the IQ9 does win, and it is boundary-independent

18.3 W whole-device is the only figure in this fleet under a 25 W battery-viable envelope. That is not a ratio that can be flattered by choosing a boundary — it is an absolute draw at the most inclusive boundary there is, measured with an external meter. The comparison devices are at 52 W (accelerator rail alone), 120–140 W and 286 W. For any deployment gated on a battery, a fanless enclosure or a thermal budget, this is the determining number, and per-token energy is not.

4. The performance model predicts the hardware to 1.1%

A roofline model of decode throughput on the Hexagon NPU, given the model's W4A16 weight footprint per token:

predicted 18.5 tok/s  vs  measured 18.3 tok/s  →  1.1% residual

Qwen3-4B W4A16 on QCS9075 Hexagon HTP measured + derived

Stated as a self-check, not as an independent validation — and the reason is worth reading, because it is the kind of thing usually left out. The model's memory-bandwidth input for the NPU (~37 GB/s) is itself back-derived from the decode throughput it then goes on to predict. The QCS9075 exposes no bandwidth counter, so no independent copy-benchmark number exists to feed in. The 1.1% therefore demonstrates that the roofline formulation is self-consistent and that decode on this device is bandwidth-bound as expected; it does not independently confirm the bandwidth figure. Calling it a validation would be circular, so this page does not.

6. Claims previously made that this work has withdrawn

Published here on purpose. A results page that never retracts anything is not being audited.

  • “8B does not fit the NPU / 4B is the ceiling” → withdrawn. 8B W4A16 runs at 11.2 tok/s on a fresh-boot idle NPU (section 2).
  • “IQ9 draws ~12 W” → withdrawn. Metered at 18.3 W; the 12 W figure was a proxy, not an instrument reading.
  • “IQ9 leads energy efficiency at 1.48 tok/J” → withdrawn. It is 0.97 tok/J once metered; GB10 leads.
  • “IQ9 wins latency by ~1000×” → withdrawn. That compared a 4B model against a 35B model — not the same workload. The honest like-for-like result is the mixed one in section 1.
  • The 24-hour aggregate as a “measured” anchor → withdrawn; relabelled illustrative (section 5).

7. How the numbers were taken

  • Valid rows, never exit codes. A benchmark cell that returns success while producing no usable output is counted as a failure. Aggregates are computed over valid samples, never over run counts or file counts.
  • The extractor is verified before any aggregate is trusted. Several apparent “model failures” in this project turned out to be response-parsing bugs — including one where a correct answer scored zero because the parser matched the echoed prompt instead of the reply. Heuristic adopted: two different models producing identical extreme scores indicates the instrument, not the models.
  • Latency is measured streamed and per request, with time-to-first-token and per-token latency reported separately, because on agentic workloads TTFT is dominated by prefill of the tool schemas rather than by the user prompt.
  • Tool-calling correctness is scored on separate axes — the right function name and the right arguments are never collapsed into one number. Calling the right tool with the wrong arguments is a wrong action, not a partial success.
  • Thermals were tracked throughout (the NPU dies expose per-die thermal zones); at this duty cycle the board peaked in the high-40s °C, so none of the above is a thermally throttled reading.
